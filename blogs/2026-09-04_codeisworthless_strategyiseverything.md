# script
## opening
hello. i want to talk to you about what's happening in software development right now. let's start by considering your businesses. by show of hands:
- how many of you have an ai strategy that your board is satisfied with?
  + keep your hands up if you are confident that your ai strategy returns value on investment?
  + keep your hands up if you collect hard evidence (metrics, kpi) supporting for that belief?"

there is a gap here. there is a gap between confidence and evidence, and it is right  there where many enterprise AI programmes get stuck.

As an industry, software development is undergoing (for the past 3 years) its most significant shift since the dawn of the internet.
yet there little tangible evidence that businesses are enjoying a positive bottom-line impact from this transformation. 
- The ambition is real. 
- The investment is real. 
- The urgency is real. but none of these add up to a strategy.

## thesis
i bring this up because too many times, ai adoption is doing the right thing for the wrong reasons -and poorly. 

too many times, rushing to adopt ai leads to measures of effort (ai adoption metrics, usage leaderboards, lines of code, number of commits/pull requests) rather than measures of impact (your product's resiliance, stability, performance, breadth of business problems tackled, the breadth of use cases and customers served).

my thesis is that with agentic ai coding tools the code matters less and less and strategy matters more and more. 
> when code becomes abundant, strategy becomes the deciding factor.

by strategy, here i  mean the layer between a vision and a plan. the vision explains why the strategy is chosen. strategy explains how the vision is to be implemented, strategy explains why the plan has been laid, the plain explains how the strategy is to be executed.
so a strategy means making hard choices (a thousand nos for every yes) that bring focus and clarity to the a.i. adoption while connecting it to meaningful bottom line business impact.

examples:
- what should we build?
- what should we not build, and it's even harder sibling: what should we stop building? what should we discard?
- where can the regained capacity and focus create business advantage?
- which technical problems are now economically viable to solve?

> another term for this is value stream management. i.e the art and practice of continually improving the value created during the delivery of software products and services by optimising end-to-end planning and holistic alignment with the purpose is to maximize value. that means increase efficiency, reduce time-to-market, enhance product quality and serve a greater customer experience.

at the surface level, ai increases speed and efficiency. that's great! but that is just spinning the wheel faster. 
i think of that speed boost like a currency. it can buy you a further reach. 

problems that for the longest time have been too involved to take on, now become possible to tackle: 
- legacy code modernisation, 
- technical debt payback before software assets becomes a legacy liability
- documentation automation. 
  - better documentation coverage, 
  - documentation kept up to date, 
- comprehensive testing,
- more test case scenarios, 
- and previously unviable product projects turn into strategic investment areas.
- experimentation with novel new features, 
  - research into expanded use cases, 
  - new markets

just think of whatever it is in your sdlc that you would like to improve, and have wanted to improve for a while, but never gets prioritised. this single important, but never urgent task. it's too much work, takes too much time. 
what if ai can give you more time, can reduce the amount of effort required? what opportunities for improvements could that bring into reach? what new value can 

you don't need to limit the impact of ai to spinning the same wheel faster. we can expand the wheel.

the focus on speed and efficiency makes ai adoption a cost savings exercise. cost savings are great. for one thing, they are measureable. you have the baseline, and if that sinks any closer to zero, your cfo will love you for it. it's great.

what's even better than saving money? maybe making money? 

by expanding what you can deliver, by increasing output quality, and appeal, ai adoption could instead be a growth story.

of course, it is not either/or. every business must in reality balance both. you still have the day-to-day (business as usual) optimisations. 
it's a balance of productivity and possibility. my point here, is to not lose sight of the possibilities. to think beyond efficiency to what it can buy you.

i also want to tackle one particular aspect of the cost story. and that is the -in my opinion- wrong headed talk of ai agents replacing 

this is a description of a.i. adoption as swapping out carbon based programmers for silicon based agents. i don't find the argument as convinceing as so many do. 
take two businesses in the same sector. both have the same staff costs and revenue, so share the same thin margin. 

company a adopts a.i. and goes so well, they managed cut down their staff by half. costs go down, without productivity budging at all. margins go up. happy times. 

but their competitor, b, instead retained their staff, now empowered  ai agents. by connecting the adoption with strategic goals, they were able to extend their reach, conquering new markets, growing their market presence, customising user experience, inventing new features and solutions.

both are success stories. which company does your vision document describe?

i am betting on the org with empowered employees over the one leaner team.

1. ai creates speed and efficiency.
  + AI makes producing software faster.
  + there is currently very little evidence AI delivering value faster.
  + First, controlled experiments show that large task-level gains are possible. In a GitHub Copilot experiment, participants completed a standardised JavaScript task 55.8% faster with access to Copilot. 
  + Second, more realistic studies show that the gains are highly context-dependent. A 2025 METR randomised trial involving experienced developers working on mature open-source repositories found that developers took 19% longer when AI tools were allowed. AI can reduce typing and searching while increasing prompting, waiting, reviewing, and correction.
  + Third, organisational data suggests that individual productivity and delivery performance are not the same thing. DORA’s 2025 research, based on nearly 5,000 technology professionals, found that more than 80% perceived productivity gains, while AI adoption had a positive relationship with throughput and product performance but continued to have a negative relationship with delivery stability.
  + Agentic AI can inspect a repository, form an implementation plan, modify multiple files, run tests, interpret failures, and iterate.
  + The speed capability is real. The business outcome is conditional.
2. speed is a currency, not the outcome.
3. organisations can spend it on doing the same work faster—or invest it in a larger opportunity set.
4. poor adoption measures activity and effort: usage, commits, pull requests, lines of code.
5. strategic adoption measures impact: resilience, stability, performance, debt reduction, documentation coverage, quality, and new solutions delivered.
6. ai adoption should be married to the strategy to drive growth, not merely to reduce costs.
