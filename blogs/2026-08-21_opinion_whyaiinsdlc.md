title: Why bring AI into your SDLC?
Note: The following conversation never happened. Not like this. It is almost entirely fictional. Any resemblance to persons, living or not, is purely conincidental. Or unexpectedly clever writing. Nevertheless, the names have been changed to protect the guilty parties. Just in case.
---

Percy: I’d like to ask a stupid question.

Cassandra: Here we go again.

Percy: *Why* should a dev team bring AI into their software development life cycle?

Cassandra: Seriously? How about "for all the obvious reasons"?

Percy: Humour me. 

Cassandra: Sheesh. Well, unless you’ve been living upside-down in a hole in the ground, you will have heard what agentic coding tools can do now. They’ve transformed software development forever. Not using them is just limiting yourself.

Percy: I don’t disagree. But that's not the reason. *Why*, precisely?

Cassandra: Fine. Because if you don’t, your competitors will move your cheese. And eat it.

Percy: Hype cycle buy-in? FOMO? Of all people, I know *you* don’t believe in that. You zig when they zag. So humour me: Picture an engineering head whose team still writes everything by hand. They’re highly capable and current on best practice. Pipelines are tested and quality-assured end-to-end. Uptime is within SLA, bug volume is tolerable, workload is stable. The bus chugs along the happy path. Why should they adopt AI tooling, and risk dropping a spanner into the clockwork?

Cassandra: Wait. *Where* did you find this platonic ideal of a developer team? In Rivendell? But fine, I’ll play along. Speed. That's why. Incredible, screaming speed. AI writes code way faster than any person, even your soft-fingered fairies. Or do your elongated hobbits not track their DORA metrics? Deployment frequency up, lead time down, recovery quicker. It's a no-brainer.

Percy: I was afraid you’d say that.

Cassandra: Duh. Because I’m right... Right?

Percy: You are not wrong. Coding speed is the first benefit every team sees. Enthusiasts, sceptics and detractors all agree on that, though the detractors use less flattering terms. But look at the team, elves if you like. Speed isn’t their problem. They're already shipping. Going faster down the same road doesn’t take them anywhere new.

Cassandra: I don't get your point? What's wrong with speed and efficiency?

Percy: Speed isn’t the point. Speed is a currency. The question is what you buy with it.

Cassandra: Oh, I know what you buy with it. More features. Or cost-saving headcount reductions. Management sees the team get X% faster, and by next quarter that X% is in the roadmap. And all that code you got faster? Formulaic. Mediocre. It runs, maybe. But you’re borrowing time it against tomorrow’s tech debt. Isn't that why DORA research found AI adoption made delivery less stable?

Percy: That is my point, exactly.

Cassandra: That I’m right twice?

Percy: That if we don’t consciously decide how to spend time savings, they get spent for us. On volume. On more of the same. On spinning the same wheel faster and faster. And, with more wobble. That’s the default, and it’s why so many AI adoptions feel underwhelming. 

Cassandra: Sure, and how would you spend those savings differently?

Percy: On all the things no team ever has budget for: Watching what users actually do in production, and acting on it. On the test suite expansions you’ve planned for years. On the legacy module nobody dares touch. Triaging the bug backlog properly instead of skimming it. The edge cases. The docs. On growing the wheel, not just spinning it faster. Code generation is one link in a long chain. Most of the value is in the rest of it.

Cassandra: Won't the competitors eat your cheese?

Percy: They’re all getting faster. That’s inflation. Raw speed buys you less every year. What lasts is what you built with it.

Cassandra: Meanwhile, the world fills up with mediocre code?

Percy: Let's separate fine craftsmanship and fitness-for-purpose. Beautifully hand-crafted code that only its author can maintain slides into legacy, too. It just takes longer to admit it. Think at the level of output and outcomes, not at the level of the code. If  time saved on coding is spent on impact review, edge case handlings, documentation, strengthening the undercarriege. That's how AI adoption can lead to quality increases. Real quality, measured in terms of outcomes, in real-life reliability. Speed you reinvest in reliability compounds. Speed spent on feature bloat is gone by the end of the quarter.

Cassandra: And you're saying that's why pointy-eared programmers want AI?

Percy: They shouldn't be adopting AI just to be faster. They should adopt it to achieve greater things.

Cassandra: So your grand vision is that fey folk use AI to... complete their documentation coverage?

Percy: Well, yes...

Cassandra: Elves writing documentation? Now that’s fantasy.
